The Second Cup

I still make two cups some mornings. Not because I forget - because I remember too well. The second one goes cold on the counter, and I let it, the way you used to let me talk too long about nothing. Grief is not the absence of you. It is the habit of you, still reaching for the second cup.
From Where You Are Now

The Night You Said It

You said it in the kitchen, of all places, with the tap still running and a tea towel in your hand. You said it gently, which was worse. I remember thinking someone should turn the tap off, and that it couldn't be me, because I had forgotten how hands work.
From Still Learning to Leave

The Orange

The train was late and packed, and you had the only seat across from mine, and a paperback, and an orange. You peeled it in one long curl without looking up, then held out half as if we'd known each other for years. I have been given diamonds since. Nothing has ever come close to that orange.
From You Still Give Me Butterflies

About

Janice Kingsley

Janice Kingsley writes gentle, honest poems for the biggest moments of the heart. Her poems are about the small, ordinary things that loss and love change: the second cup, the coat on the hook, the side of the bed, the song you cannot skip.

Where You Are Now is her first collection. Still Learning to Leave and You Still Give Me Butterflies complete the series, Gentle Poems for the Heart.

If you are struggling, you do not have to carry it alone. UK and Ireland: Samaritans, call 116 123, any time, day or night. USA: call or text 988. Bereavement support in the UK: Cruse, 0808 808 1677.
